# Fiction's Deadliest Characters

A 60-second Higgsfield production kit.

- **Format:** 9:16 vertical
- **Target length:** 60 seconds
- **Style:** dark cinematic documentary, black, charcoal and muted crimson. Non-graphic destruction. Spoiler warning in the opening frame.

This compares deaths and destructive scope. It is not a mathematically verified ranking. Some characters cause deaths through military orders, and the size of a universe is not a population count.

## Script and edit timeline

Read at about 140–150 words per minute, with brief pauses. Record the narration before setting the final cuts; the timings below are an edit guide.

## Clean voiceover copy

> How many lives can one fictional character end? The scale gets terrifying.
>
> Darrow, the Reaper of Mars. One attack on Ganymede led to roughly ten million deaths.
>
> Eren Yeager. The Rumbling killed eighty percent of humanity in his world.
>
> AM left only five humans alive. Almost everyone else became a victim of its hatred.
>
> Paul Atreides. The war fought in his name killed sixty-one billion people.
>
> Galactus consumes worlds. His total is unknown, but inhabited planets have been among his meals.
>
> Zeno erased an entire future timeline. That's erased reality, not a measured death toll.
>
> Anti-Monitor destroyed universes across an infinite multiverse. Ordinary numbers stop being useful.
>
> Who belongs on this list?

## Make the images first

Make seven separate vertical stills. Use a character reference if the selected model supports it. Darrow's appearance should follow a chosen book description or a consistent reference, not an invented "official" movie appearance. A good still saves you an expensive video reroll.

Append this style direction to each image prompt:

> Vertical 9:16 cinematic illustration, charcoal black and muted crimson palette, monumental scale, controlled lighting, strong central subject, clear lower third for captions, no text, no logos, no gore.

### Seven starting-image prompts

1. **Darrow**
   > Darrow, the Reaper of Mars from Red Rising, a tall scarred young warrior with golden eyes and long tawny hair, wearing dark futuristic armor, viewed from behind in three-quarter profile overlooking a ruined orbital shipyard above a moon. Broken station sections hang against space. Somber documentary composition, no sword action.

   Settle his appearance with a reference before animating anything.

2. **Eren / Rumbling**
   > Attack on Titan's Rumbling, low wide view from an empty ruined coastal city toward a line of towering skinless Wall Titans emerging from dense steam, distant rib-like silhouette of Eren's Founding Titan behind them. Monumental scale, anatomically consistent giants, no visible victims.

   Keep the number of foreground Titans small so the model can maintain their shapes.

3. **AM**
   > AM from I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream represented as a vast underground supercomputer, black machinery extending into darkness, a single red optical light, oppressive industrial scale, abandoned human world, no human face, no lettering.

   Add the five survivor dots in the editor, not through generated imagery.

4. **Paul**
   > Paul Atreides from Dune, dark-haired young desert emperor in a stillsuit and black cloak, blue-within-blue eyes, standing motionless above a vast desert city, distant banners and ships, grave expression, cinematic literary interpretation.

   No crowd close-ups or lip movement.

5. **Galactus**
   > Marvel comics Galactus, recognizable purple angular helmet and cosmic armor, immense figure towering beside an inhabited planet, a thin luminous energy stream connects the planet to his hand, stars in deep black space, planet on lower right and face on upper left.

   Use the restrained palette while keeping the recognizable purple accents.

6. **Zeno**
   > Zeno from Dragon Ball Super, recognizable small blue and purple character in ceremonial clothing, centered and expressionless against a black cosmic background, subtle luminous aura, faithful stylized proportions, stark contrast between innocent appearance and cosmic scale.

   Keep the stylized character instead of forcing a realistic human face.

7. **Anti-Monitor**
   > DC comics Anti-Monitor, massive armored cosmic figure with recognizable mechanical face and helmet, centered in dark space, three distant luminous universe-like spheres around him, antimatter darkness creeping toward the spheres, crimson edge lighting.

   The spheres are visual symbols of universes, not an accurate physical diagram.

## Animate only these three images

Use image-to-video, one shot per generation. Prefer five seconds if the chosen model offers it. Use the lowest acceptable preview resolution. Compare the displayed price across available standard and fast models with the same settings; no model is permanently the cheapest.

**Clip 1: Rumbling**
> Preserve the uploaded composition and Titan anatomy. Locked low camera. The foreground Titans make one slow forward step as steam drifts sideways. Distant Founder stays nearly motionless. No cuts, no camera orbit, no new creatures, no text, no gore.

**Clip 2: Galactus**
> Preserve Galactus's face, helmet, armor and the planet's shape. Locked camera. The existing energy stream slowly brightens and the planet's city lights fade. Galactus remains almost motionless. No planet explosion, no cuts, no extra limbs, no text.

**Clip 3: Anti-Monitor**
> Preserve the uploaded figure and framing. Locked camera. A dark antimatter wave slowly passes over the three luminous spheres, dimming them one by one until they vanish. The armored figure stays motionless. No cuts, no new objects, no text.

**Fallback** for any failed or expensive animation: use the approved still with a slow editor zoom and a sound effect. Every scene is designed to work without video generation.

## Higgsfield workflow

1. Open <https://higgsfield.ai> and check your credit balance and which models your plan covers with Unlimited or free generations. Those benefits are specific to your plan.
2. Build a rough edit from placeholder images and record the narration. Decide whether the pace works before paying for any video.
3. Create one still at a time, requesting one result per run. Fix composition and identity at this stage.
4. Choose image-to-video for the Rumbling still. Upload it, paste the matching animation prompt, set vertical format, five seconds when available, and preview resolution. Turn off generated audio if it's optional; narration and effects are added later.
5. Check the exact credit cost on the Generate button and log it before running. Make one clip only.
6. If that clip is usable, do Galactus and Anti-Monitor the same way.
7. Download the results and assemble them in an editor such as CapCut or DaVinci Resolve. Put all names, counts and percentages in editor text. Use one narration track.
8. Export the finished timeline at 1080 × 1920 if your editor supports it. Upscaling generated clips is optional and may cost extra; exporting at 1080p doesn't add detail that a low-resolution source lacks.

Higgsfield's official help says Unlimited and included free generations apply on its website only. Automated generations through MCP, CLI, Canvas and Supercomputer deduct credits even where a web-only benefit exists. The exact cost depends on model, duration and resolution, and is displayed before generation.

## Credit guardrails

- **Recommended baseline:** at most 7 paid still-image runs + 3 paid five-second video runs.
- **Reserve:** up to 2 extra image corrections + 1 video retry across the whole project.
- **Hard maximum:** 9 image runs + 4 video runs. The reserve is not something to spend automatically.

If one image costs **I** credits and one five-second video costs **V** credits:

- Baseline estimate: **7I + 3V**
- Maximum with reserve: **9I + 4V**
- With mixed models, add up each displayed price instead.

*Illustration only, not a Higgsfield quote:* if I = 2 and V = 20, the baseline is 74 credits and the maximum is 98. Use your actual displayed costs.

Set a total ceiling before running anything, for example 100 credits for a first pass. That number is a suggested personal budget, not a promise that every model combination fits it. Continue only if the remaining essential shots fit within the remaining budget; if not, replace animations with stills.

## Editing and sound

- Start with a low pulse, then add a heavier impact on each character change.
- Drop the music briefly for "sixty-one billion" and "entire future timeline."
- Fade most sound out as Anti-Monitor's universes disappear.
- Use original or properly licensed music.
- Keep captions away from the bottom edge and platform buttons.
- Use "TOTAL UNKNOWN" instead of inventing a number.
- Add this footer to the caption: "Fictional destruction comparison. Includes indirect deaths and later-reversed events. Not an exact ranking."

**Suggested post caption:**

## Fact notes and sources

- **Darrow:** a quoted passage from *Iron Gold* reports 100,000 deaths in the dockyards and 10 million when wreckage fell on New Troy, rounded here to roughly ten million. The passage is reproduced in a reader discussion rather than a publisher-hosted excerpt, so check it against your edition before publishing an exact figure.
  <https://www.reddit.com/r/redrising/comments/1twe8uz/ganymede_morning_star/>
- **Eren:** the 80% refers to his fictional world, not Earth's current population. Don't convert it to a modern-Earth number.
  <https://attackontitan.fandom.com/wiki/Rumbling>
- **AM:** the short story leaves five humans alive after AM exterminates the rest.
  <https://www.litcharts.com/lit/i-have-no-mouth-and-i-must-scream/themes/humanity-vs-technology>
- **Paul:** 61 billion is the war's toll stated in *Dune Messiah*, not the number Paul personally killed.
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune_Messiah>
- **Galactus:** no verified lifetime total, and not every planet he consumes is inhabited.
  <https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/heralds-of-galactus>
  <https://www.marvel.com/characters/ego/in-comics/>
- **Zeno:** Dragon Ball's official summary confirms the erasure of Trunks' future timeline. Zamasu had already killed much of its life, so don't count an assumed full population for every erased universe.
  <https://en.dragon-ball-official.com/news/01_900.html>
- **Anti-Monitor:** DC describes the reduction of the infinite multiverse to one universe. Its later restoration complicates permanent-death totals, so "multiversal destruction" is safer than a numerical infinity counter.
  <https://www.dc.com/blog/2021/08/16/ask-the-question-how-many-people-died-in-each-crisis>
- **Higgsfield credits and web-only benefits** (checked October 6, 2026):
  <https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/credits/how-credits-work>
  <https://higgsfield.ai/blog/why-ai-video-credits-run-out>
